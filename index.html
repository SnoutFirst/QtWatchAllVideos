What it's for

Its primary intended usage is browsing 4chan /gif/ threads — where a thread is basically a long list of .webm and .gif links and you just want to sit back and let them play.

That said, the extraction is deliberately generic: it will work on any site that serves its videos as plain <video> / <source> elements or as links ending in a video extension (.webm, .mp4, .gif, .mov, .mkv, .ogg, and friends, plus the usual legacy formats).

What it will not do

It does not execute a site's player logic. Extraction is done on the static HTML only, so it won't work on sites that use fancy JavaScript players to load their videos — YouTube is the obvious example, but plenty of others are in the same boat (anything with blob/MSE streaming, DRM, or an API-driven player).

CAPTCHAs

It can get past a Cloudflare or CAPTCHA interstitial — but not on its own. The real page loads in the embedded browser first, and you pass the challenge by hand; the video scan then runs against the page you ended up on. There is no solver, no cookie farming and no headless trickery: the human does the one bit that needs a human, and everything after that is automatic.

Features

  • Watch all videos in order — every match is collected from the page (deduplicated) and played sequentially, advancing automatically when each one ends.
  • Play order — Forward (default), Shuffled, or Reversed, chosen from the drop-down arrow next to the Watch All Videos button (Ctrl+S / Ctrl+R), or from the URL field's right-click menu.
  • Animated GIF support — .gif files are downloaded and played as animated GIFs with proper scaling. A GIF that loops for more than a few seconds is treated as "finished" and auto-advances to the next video.
  • Resume where you left off — recent history is saved locally (per thread URL, in a QSettings INI file under the platform's app-data directory), so re-opening a thread picks up at the last video you were watching. History entries older than a year are pruned automatically.
  • Minimal, out-of-the-way UI — the controls panel and the mouse cursor hide themselves after 1.5 seconds of inactivity and come back as soon as you move the mouse.
  • Single click to pause, double click for fullscreen — click anywhere on the video to pause/resume; double-click (or F) toggles fullscreen.
  • Keyboard shortcuts
    • Space — play / pause
    • N or Enter — next video
    • P — previous video
    • ← — jump back 5 seconds (stops at the start of the video)
    • → — jump forward 5 seconds (past the end, it moves on to the next video)
    • M — mute / unmute
    • F — toggle fullscreen
    • Esc — leave fullscreen
  • Previous / Next buttons plus a current / total counter.
  • Crazy-clicking friendly — a small race guard keeps an auto-advance and a panicked Next click from skipping a video. Spamming Next still works at full speed.
  • Interruptible downloads — switching videos while a GIF is still downloading aborts the pending transfer instead of letting the old one pop up later.
  • Remembers your window — size and position are restored on the next launch (maximized on first run).
